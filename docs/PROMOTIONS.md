# Promotions, featured products and weekly sales (schema v7)

## Pricing contract

A promotion is one fixed **integer-cent USD sale price** for one counted product, with a UTC start and end. The manager editor accepts decimal USD text (for example `2.49`) and converts digits exactly to cents; it never rounds floating-point input. A sale must be positive and strictly below that product's regular price. Percentages, coupons and stacking are not supported.

The interval is **start-inclusive and end-exclusive**: a sale starting at Monday 00:00 UTC is active at that instant; one ending at the following Monday 00:00 UTC is already inactive then. Adjacent windows are allowed. Overlapping noncancelled windows for the same product are refused in the immediate transaction and by SQLite triggers, including concurrent requests. Cancelled rows and audit history remain recorded. Active, Upcoming, Expired and Cancelled are computed from the Store clock; unavailable products are labelled separately.

Regular catalog price remains independent from the effective selling price. The same resolver supplies storefront cards, weekly offers, basket totals and checkout. Checkout captures one transaction-clock instant, validates the reviewed quote, then writes the effective unit price to both the original receipt and initial working allocation. A 15-minute reservation holds **stock, not price**. If a sale starts, ends, is edited or is cancelled before submission, the customer must review current prices and submit again. A last-crossed-boundary marker also invalidates an old quote if an entire short sale begins and ends between review and submission, even when the total returns to the old amount. Rejected price quotes leave live holds untouched.

Promotion mutations advance the product's price version, but not its stock or catalog version. Featured changes do not change any price version or quote. A regular-price edit is rejected if it would meet or undercut any active/upcoming sale price; unit/basis/quantity-step changes are rejected while such an offer remains. Adjust or cancel the conflicting offer first. Valid higher regular prices and harmless description/category/art edits do not change the scheduled fixed sale amount. Archived products and gram products cannot be promoted or newly featured. Archive suppresses existing merchandising without deleting it; restoring an eligible product resumes its unexpired schedule and featured flag. Archived flags remain removable in Featured management.

## Placed receipts and working-order overrides

Original placed receipts, recorded working prices and final totals are never recomputed from current promotions. Existing working lines, including zero-quantity tombstones added back later, keep their recorded unit rate. A product first added or substituted into a working order snapshots the current effective rate. The manager's catalog quote includes sale identity/version, price version and crossed-boundary marker; a changed quote requires review before any stock, working-line or audit mutation. Replacing into a product already present uses its retained recorded rate. Existing scoped ownership, command replay, stock disposition, assignments and fulfillment closure remain unchanged.

## Manager and storefront

Promotions is a task-focused workspace with dedicated Sales and Featured screens, bounded search/state filters, 20-row pages, dedicated sale editors and a selected featured-product editor. Lists and mutations preserve their navigation context. All controls have ordinary HTML forms; HTMX progressively enhances them. Manager authorization, CSRF, optimistic sale/feature versions and bounded escaped drafts apply to each write. Recoverable errors retain attempted editable values beside refreshed authoritative state; they do not silently save a stale form.

The homepage leads with up to three currently active sales, real regular/sale prices and **Shop all sales**. Search, department and sales-only filtering are retained after adding to the basket. The separate Featured strip shows up to four eligible counted products that are not already in the circular. Its total count and See all featured filter still include every eligible choice; the compact circular footer keeps that filter accessible when all featured picks are already on sale. Featured and sales filters combine with search and department. Both use the existing grocery-circular palette and illustrations. When no offer is active, the homepage says so; it does not invent a discount or keep showing an expired price. Sold-out sale products remain clearly labelled and cannot be added.

The compact circular uses a yellow headline column and one horizontal row of up to three offers on desktop, with blue rules, red prices and the original local product illustrations. Headline type and art are bounded to keep the actual catalog in view. A compact basket/footer bar replaces the tall side receipt. One or two offers use the same band without an empty lower row; more than three retain the true live count and a path to the full sales filter. Narrow screens use a short headline and compact offer rows, keeping each price, UTC end time and basket action together. The zero-offer edition explains that only everyday prices are available. The circular and separate Featured strip do not repeat the same products, and no catalog or merchandising records are changed by that presentation choice.

Each offer has a named article and a stable add-form ID. Its button describes the product and references the visible stock state and end date; sold-out buttons are disabled. Existing CSRF, revision, filter preservation and contextual response feedback still use the same cart workflow. This presentation never creates or refreshes offers: expired or missing promotions must be reviewed through the manager's existing guarded controls.

Every successful promotion or featured command records a snapshot in `promotion_events` in the same transaction; failed audit insertion rolls the change back. Shared Stock & Activity links to the relevant sale or featured editor. Those records contain shared catalog information only; existing basket/order reasons and shopper events retain their session scope. The shared demo gate does not identify a real employee.

## Example offers and reset

Normal migration, ordinary startup and restart create **no example offers or featured choices** and never refresh old ones. In shared demo mode only, **Create example weekly sales** first shows the exact three products, regular/sale rates, UTC window and shared effect. Its protected POST requires explicit confirmation and a reviewed preview quote. It creates apples at $2.49, sourdough at $3.99 and milk at $3.29, and features those same products, for the current Monday-to-Monday UTC week.

The action requires the original known seed product IDs, names, SKUs, rates, counted units and unchanged catalog/price versions. It refuses custom, archived or weighed products, overlaps and any previous featured choice on those products. All three offers and featured choices commit atomically. It never overwrites an existing offer, edits the catalog, changes stock, clears sessions or resets an order. A unique command key makes an exact successful retry harmless. Changed preview data, a week boundary or a different reused payload requires review. Another visitor can see these shared demo offers.

An **explicitly confirmed global demo reset** uses the existing private verified-backup workflow and adds these examples to its fresh fixture after migration. Its week is taken from the reset's injected Store clock. The full previous database, including promotions, features and audit, remains in the recovery archive. Ordinary subsequent restarts preserve the fixture; they never move expired offers into a new week automatically.

Version 7 adds `promotions`, `product_features`, `promotion_events` and replay records in `promotion_commands`. Prior table contents, prices, SKUs/codes, basket holds, original receipts, working lines/tombstones, shopper assignments and audit links are preserved. No historical sale provenance is fabricated. A populated independent v6 fixture verifies upgrade, final-statement failure rollback, retry, foreign-key integrity and restart persistence. Reset tests verify complete archive recovery and deterministic UTC week boundaries.

Domain and HTTP tests exercise exact sale boundaries (including an entire interval between quotes), stale/cancelled/edited offers, overlap races, active/upcoming price-edit guards, sale checkout snapshots, new working additions versus retained prices, featured quote independence, audit rollback, no-JS/HTMX forms, CSRF, demo-only confirmed examples and filter preservation. Run `./scripts/check.sh` plus the required HTTP smoke workflow. Live desktop/mobile and physical-device visual/accessibility verification remain separate from these rendered-HTML and domain checks.

Circular-specific HTTP checks cover zero, one, two, three and four active offers, excluding upcoming/expired prices, retaining the full-sales link, submitting the actual rendered forms with and without HTMX, preserving search/department/sales/featured context, using the current selling price, and reflecting sold-out stock and exact expiry. These checks establish behavior and markup, not pixel-level layout; desktop/mobile screenshot review remains a release check.
